`boxer-seven-lead` is Greybound's original-name lead-channel target inspired by
the Bogner Shiva 20th Anniversary lead channel. It focuses on thick classic-rock
lead saturation, forward but controllable mids, high-headroom KT-style output
feel, and global presence/excursion behavior.

V1 is not a complete Shiva platform, not the clean channel, and not a
component-exact schematic clone.

## Modeling Scope

The current implementation is a tuned graybox approximation. It uses public
control/topology information plus existing Greybound amp-stage primitives:

- high-impedance input coupling,
- bright input gain that becomes less dominant as gain rises,
- cascaded tube-style lead gain,
- asymmetric cold-biased lead clipping,
- recovery/follower behavior into a three-band tone stack,
- post-tone loop/master driver,
- presence shaping,
- phase-inverter approximation,
- high-headroom KT-style push-pull current-bank response,
- excursion/sag envelope and low-frequency feel shaping,
- output coupling and low-pass filtering before cab/IR.

## Source Confidence

Public sources agree on the useful V1 target shape: the 20th Anniversary Shiva
lead channel exposes gain plus bass/middle/treble, has bright/shift/mode
voicing controls, and shares master/presence/excursion controls. Review and
modeling-community notes describe the KT88 20th Anniversary version as a
high-headroom, wide, dynamic amp with a dark/thick default lead voice and
classic-rock gain rather than extreme modern metal gain.

## Current DSP Approximation

The Rust model lives in
`core/src/amp/models/boxer_seven.rs::BoxerSevenLead`.

Control mapping:

- `volume`: lead gain into the cascade.
- `bass`: low band of the three-band graybox tone stack.
- `cut`: temporary mid control until shared amp controls expose `mid`.
- `treble`: high band of the three-band graybox tone stack.
- `drive`: V1 mode/boost aggression hypothesis; it raises clipping gain,
  compression, and upper-mid bite.
- `presence`: high-frequency feedback-style lift before the output section.
- `sag`: excursion/depth feel hypothesis; it changes low-frequency bloom and
  supply/bias softness.
- `output`: final global output gain from the runtime.

## Initial Validation

Focused tests:

```bash
cargo test -p greybound boxer_seven -- --nocapture
cargo test -p greybound circuit_descriptor::tests::boxer_seven -- --nocapture
```

The first tests verify:

- hot input stays finite,
- the mode/boost hypothesis produces measurably more nonlinear residual energy
  than the lower-aggression setting,
- the circuit descriptor exposes lead scope and a KT-style power-stage node.

Recommended first monitor render:

```bash
cargo build --release -p greybound-cli
target/release/greybound-cli --rig rigs/boxer-seven-lead.json5 --input-wav "lab/references/tone3000-inputs/Brit - Guitar.wav" --output-wav target/boxer-seven-lead-3s.wav --render-seconds 3 --sample-rate 48000 --period-size 32 --ir lab/references/tone3000-irs/celestion.wav --monitor --monitor-log target/boxer-seven-lead-3s.monitor.log
```

Initial validation is runtime-health and regression-oriented only. It does not
prove match quality against a measured Shiva 20th Anniversary capture. The next
useful experiment is a fixed DI comparison against a measured hardware capture,
NAM/Axe reference render, or a project-owned SPICE fixture for the lead tone and
mode/excursion controls.
